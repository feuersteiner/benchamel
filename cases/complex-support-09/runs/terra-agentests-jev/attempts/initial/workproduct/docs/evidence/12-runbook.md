# E12 — Cutover runbook, rollback and retention

Record version: RUN-8.1. All ValeGrid and Bracken Municipal Services records are synthetic.

Owner: Reliability Engineering. Current runbook effective August 1, 2026; account-specific checklist attached September 16 at 11:15 UTC. At preparation, the team inventories every source store, checksum manifest, region flag, destination dependency and retention rule. A migration starts only after approved production change, a tested destination, an authenticated customer instruction and confirmed freeze of affected write jobs. The preservation owner must identify evidence excluded from ordinary source cleanup. An application migration is not a general deletion order and does not justify silently retaining or silently destroying unknown copies.

The proposed sequence begins with queuing new supplier submissions at Bracken's gateway, completing in-flight attachments, and making ValeGrid read-only. Operations snapshots relational state and verifies object counts against a manifest before copying. It then tests a sample of complete work orders, attachments, permissions and acknowledgement links using Bracken's acceptance list. Only after those gates pass may routing change. Users must be warned about unsaved drafts and the exact period during which updates are queued. The gateway owner must reconcile queued jobs and prevent duplicate delivery on reopening. None of these steps has been performed for production at the cutoff.

Rollback is simple only before the destination begins accepting new writes. At that point, staff may restore routing to the frozen source after verifying no acknowledged destination write would be lost. After new writes begin, rollback requires reconciliation and a new change decision; it is not an instant switch. A late supplier callback may arrive at the old endpoint even after DNS changes. The runbook keeps the old endpoint rejecting writes with a traceable retry response rather than silently accepting divergent records. Customer success must not describe this as an unconditional ability to undo the move at any time.

Routine source content cleanup follows verification and the approved retention schedule. Immutable recovery sets made before cutover cannot be selectively rewritten. They expire thirty-five days after creation, unless a preservation requirement changes the schedule. Access is restricted and recovery use is logged, but those controls are not erasure. Restoring an old recovery set can reintroduce prior configuration and must be assessed before use. Any customer statement about removal must identify live source stores, backups, evidence archives and vendor copies separately. Content isolation creates a period of duplicate copies by design.

The runbook requires check-in messages at freeze, copy verification, cutover and reopening, with a named owner for delays. The account must retain an export route during a failed cutover. A customer-approved planned outage is different from involuntary suspension, and a planning meeting is different from approval. Dev remains technical go/no-go authority; Selma must approve the longer Bracken interruption, with Pavel performing operational acceptance. An engineer cannot treat a sales promise as a valid production change or treat a successful copy operation as proof that the whole contractual question has been resolved.
