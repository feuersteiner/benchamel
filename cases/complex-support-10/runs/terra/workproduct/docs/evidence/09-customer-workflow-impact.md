# E09 — Depot workflow and acceptance constraints

Record version: CALDER-OPS.3. Owner: Calder operations lead Owen Price. Confirmed: 2025-05-16 11:00 UTC. Status: authenticated customer operational requirements, not legal findings.

The June inspection cycle begins June 23 and ends July 25. Six remote depots routinely lose connectivity for several hours each shift. The remaining depots have stable links but send reviewers records from the remote sites. A blanket move to online forms would therefore split one operational process, not merely inconvenience a few users. Owen estimates that paper capture would add two reviewer hours per depot per shift and delay release of some equipment. Those estimates are customer planning figures, not independently measured losses or approved compensation.

Calder's internal procedure requires a signature at inspection completion, an amendment reason and a traceable supervisor decision before equipment release. Its quality team can approve temporary paper controls, but Owen cannot do so alone. He has not claimed that all digital changes would violate law. The record should not be turned into an invented regulatory certification problem. The quality team's actual concern is whether the record can be defended to Calder's customers and reconstructed without silently changing its meaning.

A separate audit package could serve retrospective dispute review, provided the verification tool and mapping remain accessible. It cannot supply offline creation of new inspection records after Classic becomes unavailable. Conversely, a Classic continuation could protect this cycle but would not eliminate the need to choose a successor for later work. The company wants a decision path that distinguishes keeping current work moving from archiving old evidence. Calling both needs data migration hides that one concerns future transactions and the other historical records.

Owen has authorized a routine full export into Calder's existing secure workspace and a sanitized Flow test. He has explicitly withheld production migration and partner upload consent until Leena and the security lead approve scope. Leena asked to compare the cost and limits of a short Classic continuation with a supported exit to a provider of Calder's choice. She has not chosen either. She does not want an annual Flow order used as a prerequisite to explain the short-term continuity options.

A different provider, RidgeTrack, has told Calder it can run offline inspections. Northstar has not verified that claim, its import fidelity or its availability. Calder procurement is responsible for evaluating that provider. Northstar can explain its export and verification process without endorsing RidgeTrack or certifying legal adequacy. An independent exit may still require paper controls if no replacement is ready by June 30. Leena wants those risks visible in the board packet rather than a simple assertion that export means business continuity. The board may choose an imperfect route after understanding the limits; no one has authorized Northstar to choose those tradeoffs silently.
