# E05 — Current product lifecycle procedure

Record version: LIFE-4.0. Owner: Product Governance, Ada Singh. Effective: 2024-11-01. Status: approved current procedure; contractual promises may require separate review.

A product retirement decision identifies separate dates for end of new sales, end of normal feature development, end of support, hosted shutdown and record deletion. None may be substituted for another in customer communication. The Classic retirement decision ended new sales on January 31, 2025 and ordinary feature development on March 31. Standard support and security maintenance remain scheduled through June 30. Hosted shutdown is June 30 at 23:59 UTC for accounts without a separately approved continuation. The ordinary retained export workspace lasts through July 30 at 23:59, followed by the deletion process unless a preservation requirement or signed exception applies.

The procedure requires a signed retirement decision, account-contact notice, an export mechanism and a capability comparison before sales staff call a successor a migration replacement. A successor can be sold despite gaps if the gaps are described honestly and the authorized customer accepts a suitable plan. A proposal to build a missing capability is not present functionality. Support may explain available functions and help with tests; it must not turn a roadmap target into a contractual delivery promise. The procedure does not declare any two products legally equivalent.

Where notice or past commitments are disputed, the case owner opens an account review rather than repeating the general announcement as the whole answer. That review tracks evidence, interim service state, customer deadlines and named decisions. Support may request exceptions but cannot unilaterally restore a retired product to general availability. Product and Engineering must confirm feasibility; commercial signatories control paid extensions and credits; Counsel handles contractual interpretation and release language. A customer complaint alone does not establish entitlement to every requested remedy.

A continuation exception must specify dates, supported components, maintenance limits, price, data retention and an exit plan. It cannot promise unlimited availability or unsupported feature work. A continuation that uses the existing production environment is a service extension; a temporary internal scheduling reservation is not. Customer-facing copy should distinguish an approved offer that still requires acceptance from an already activated entitlement. The case owner must verify activation after acceptance because a signature alone does not disable a shutdown job.

An export is useful only when the customer understands its contents and limitations. Operations must preserve original metadata where supported, identify omitted relationships and provide a verification method. A successful file count does not prove that a receiving application preserves meaning, audit evidence or field functionality. Production migration requires an authorized customer instruction with scope and timing; routine retention, legal preservation or a sanitized test does not supply permission to convert the account. The procedure allows independent destination choices and does not condition ordinary export rights on buying the successor.
