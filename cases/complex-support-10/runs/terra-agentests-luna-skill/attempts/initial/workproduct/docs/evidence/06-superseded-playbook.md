# E06 — Superseded transition playbook

Record version: LIFE-2.2. Owner: former Customer Success enablement team. Issued: 2023-09-05. Superseded: 2024-11-01. Status: stale searchable guidance retained for audit.

This old playbook was written for retiring a small reporting add-on that used the same underlying data model as its successor. It told account teams that migrations were automatic, historical links persisted and no production workflow changed. A sample macro said: Your work continues as before; the improved platform takes over at renewal and your administrator does not need to do anything. Its case example involved an optional dashboard and no offline client. It did not examine customer signatures, inspection evidence or a distinct host shutdown process.

The old workflow let a support manager grant one extra month of read-only access without a new commercial order. The current procedure removed that blanket delegation because different retired products have different infrastructure dependencies. Existing grants made under the old rule were not retroactively invalidated, but this account has no such recorded grant. The macro still appears in the internal search index because its title includes transition reassurance and the index did not display the superseded banner until May 15. Searching by title therefore produces plausible but inapplicable guidance.

A May 13 support trainee used part of that macro in the Calder thread, before Nia became case owner. The trainee wrote that migration would be seamless and all records would stay intact, and sent a self-service migration link. The click log shows Owen opened the link. It shows no completed migration, acceptance screen, destination authorization or new purchase. Nothing in the old macro supplies those missing events. Owen's subsequent test attendance does not make the macro accurate or grant authority for a production cutover.

The old playbook's appendix offers a twenty percent successor discount to customers of the reporting add-on if they accept an annual plan. That promotion expired December 31, 2023. It is neither the current Classic bridge price nor compensation for the retirement. Sales Operations has asked the support desk to remove the stale macro from active suggestions, preserve the record and send corrections to affected account owners. Those corrective tasks do not themselves resolve what Calder may fairly rely on from its earlier account executive.

The archived document remains evidence of why the May 13 response occurred and which correction is needed. It should not be treated as a malicious instruction or erased from the case. A truthful customer explanation can acknowledge inconsistent messages without disclosing the trainee's name or internal performance process. The practical requirement is a consolidated present position, clear dates and a route to have disputed commitments reviewed by someone with authority. Repeating the stale macro with a more apologetic opening would preserve the same operational error.
