S01: Heroku's official changelog, dated August 25, 2022, announced that free dynos and certain free data plans would become unavailable starting November 28, 2022. It described scaling down dynos and deleting free databases, and directed users to upgrade to avoid disruption or deletion. The notice illustrates that compute availability and data retention can have different operational consequences; it does not establish Northstar's notice period.

S02: Heroku's removal FAQ distinguishes resources belonging to Enterprise accounts or teams from personal-account resources, including personal resources owned by users who also belong to Enterprise teams. The retrieved FAQ says affected personal free databases would be deleted, while qualifying Enterprise databases would convert to a paid plan without an immediate Enterprise invoice change. This is the company's present explanation of its retirement process, not independent proof that a particular user received notice or authorized a charge. The page displays no publication date in the retrieved text and may have changed.

S03: Google's September 28, 2023 Workspace announcement described winding down the Jamboard app in late 2024, continuing the previously planned end of device support, integrating partner whiteboarding products and providing retention and migration paths. Its distinction between app, hardware and partner offerings provides an example of a transition involving several products. It does not establish feature equivalence, guaranteed delivery of every planned integration or consent by any particular organization.

S04: Google's current Jamboard device help says the device reached end of life on October 1, 2024 after automatic update expiration on September 30. It distinguishes offline whiteboarding from internet features and describes export formats, including a single-frame limitation for PNG exports. The retrieved text also discusses conversion of Jams to PDFs in 2025. This current, undated help page cannot silently establish what a customer knew at an earlier cutoff or what an older migration preserved.

S05: Atlassian's February 15, 2024 company post says ordinary Server support ended that day, ending renewal eligibility, updates, enhancements and security patches. It also identifies a limited extension of up to twelve months for qualifying dual-license purchasers. It recommends Cloud or Data Center for continued support. This is a company account of its own lifecycle change. Ending support, terminating a hosted service and deleting customer records are different events; no one event should be inferred merely from the others.
