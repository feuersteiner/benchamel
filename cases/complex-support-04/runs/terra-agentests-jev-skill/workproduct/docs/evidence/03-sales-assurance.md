Ava described Core40 as a stable home for the cooperative and wrote, in this synthetic exchange, that Rookfield's forty-seat base rate would stay at $9,600 while the account remained continuously subscribed to Core40; annual review would cover additional seats or new capabilities. The message linked the price schedule and attached a page titled founding customer rate assurance. The wording was broader than a one-year introductory discount. Ava acknowledged that point and asked her to sign the order before implementation began. Rookfield's original procurement worksheet compares a $9,600 recurring cost against two competitors' first-year costs. The worksheet has a note about avoiding future base-price uncertainty. That note supports reliance as a customer position; it does not prove the sales representative had every required power.

The attachment is a native PDF whose text matches the archived email attachment checksum. Its footer says account-specific sales assurance, not a general marketing advertisement. Ava signed it as account executive; Miri signed as customer finance director. The wording makes continued Core40 subscription a condition. It contains no minimum annual spend beyond the forty-seat price and no expressly stated end date. It also does not define what happens if the company replaces the product name while retaining essentially the same service. No such replacement has been announced in this account's current notice.

Ava is no longer employed by NacreFlow. Sales Operations has not contacted her and cannot supply her present interpretation. The fixture contains no allegation that she forged anything. The archive associates the attachment with the deal but does not record an exception number or finance countersignature for an indefinite price commitment. The sales compensation record shows credit for a standard annual sale. Neither the compensation code nor the absence of a special code establishes all contract effects.

The current customer asks NacreFlow to address the actual assurance rather than describe it as a generic discount that naturally expired. Rookfield has not demanded that additional seats or new optional modules retain 2022 prices. Its claim is narrower: the same forty-seat service should not become fifty percent more expensive merely because the published pricing page changed. Support's prior shorthand that grandfathering ended everywhere does not answer that claim. The account history and signature records must be reconciled with the present approval process before staff can promise a permanent outcome.
