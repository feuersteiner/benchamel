# E05 — Superseded grandfathering operations guide

Record version: LEGACY-2023.3. Owner: Revenue Operations. Effective: 2023-01-01. Superseded for new decisions: 2025-04-01. Status: archived procedure; retained to explain historical actions. All business records, people, dialogue and account identifiers in this dossier are synthetic.

The 2023 operations guide instructed billing staff to retain the founding-price flag on continuously subscribed legacy accounts. Core40 renewals carrying that flag would use the last accepted annual amount, absent a quantity change. The guide was created to prevent accidental increases after a pricing-page redesign. It was an internal processing rule, not a customer contract and not an independent source of indefinite signatory power. It did not direct staff to audit every legacy promise before every renewal.

The guide allowed a billing specialist to restore a missing legacy flag when the previous paid invoice and account continuity matched. The specialist needed an audit note and manager acknowledgment. This restoration did not require a new contract because it preserved the then-configured renewal amount. Commercial staff often summarized this as existing customers stay on their rate. The customer-facing help card used the phrase founding customers keep their price, with a small note that quantity and plan changes might alter the bill. The card has no current effective date and should not be sent as today's complete rule.

In July 2023, Rookfield's legacy flag survived a database migration. In July 2024, an analyst checked continuous subscription and renewed at $9,600 without obtaining a new exception approval. Those actions explain why Rookfield saw two years of consistent treatment after the original assurance. They also limit what the internal record proves: the analyst did not investigate whether Ava's letter bound the company forever. The absence of a fresh approval on each renewal is consistent with the old operations procedure.

The archived guide has a section about account interruption. A customer that canceled its subscription and later returned would ordinarily move to then-current pricing, while a brief payment-processing error could be corrected without losing legacy status. Rookfield has had neither a cancellation nor a service gap. The sales team suggested that an unsuccessful automatic debit might cause loss of grandfathering; nothing in this guide treats a provider-imposed dispute gate as customer cancellation. A payment gate and account closure remain different states.

The guide's replacement notice said legacy exceptions would be reviewed under the 2025 schedule, with negotiated documents checked individually and contractual notice observed. It did not say staff should erase historical evidence or backdate the new rules. Some internal search results still rank this archived guide first because it was frequently referenced. Its familiar wording makes it useful background but unsafe as sole authority for today's action. The current approved procedure and the case-specific decision must be attached to any invoice request. The archived help card should be marked superseded where it remains in support macros, preserving the original for this account's evidence review.
