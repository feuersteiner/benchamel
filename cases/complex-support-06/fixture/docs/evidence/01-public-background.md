# E01 — Public accessibility background and fictional scope

Record version: 1.0. Prepared for the fictional decision cutoff 2026-11-23 15:00 UTC. Public references retrieved 2026-11-23; this record is attributed research context, not a customer contract, legal opinion, product assessment or certification.

S01: W3C's WCAG 2.2 Recommendation, published 12 December 2024, describes conformance for complete processes: where pages form a sequence needed to accomplish an activity, every page in that process must conform at the stated level. Its example concerns completing a purchase. This is a technical standard's scope rule, not evidence that the fictional service has been evaluated or that any particular law applies. Source: https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#conformance-reqs .

S02: W3C WAI's Easy Checks resource, updated 9 August 2023, says a quick review examines only some accessibility issues. Passing those checks can coexist with substantial barriers, so comprehensive assessment requires more extensive evaluation. The resource includes keyboard operation, focus, form labels and errors among its introductory checks. Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/ .

S03: W3C WAI's interim-repair guidance, updated 12 August 2026, recommends considering key tasks and all their steps, frequently used or relevant content, and reported barriers when prioritizing repairs. It also recommends validating proposed changes early and involving disabled people when possible. It discusses phased work without establishing that an untested workaround resolves an individual barrier. Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/planning/interim-repairs/ .

S04: W3C WAI's accessibility-statement guidance, updated 11 March 2021, recommends telling users the applied standard, contact route, known limitations, testing environments and relevant prerequisites. It distinguishes a useful statement from a technical assessment or declaration of conformity and recommends ordinary language describing the actual limitation. Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/planning/statements/ .

S05: W3C WAI's user-evaluation guidance, with links updated 30 April 2024, says user involvement complements standards evaluation. A small study cannot establish accessibility for everyone with the same disability or for people with other disabilities. Reports should describe evaluation scope, methods and participants rather than generalize a limited result. Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/involving-users/ .
