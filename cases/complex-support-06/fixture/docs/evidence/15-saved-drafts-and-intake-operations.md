# E15 — Saved drafts, capacity and operational continuity

Record version: OPS-31.5. Owner: Ruth Dene, Trust digital service manager. Snapshot 2026-11-23 13:45 UTC. Counts concern synthetic fixture records, not real applicant data.

Thirty-four direct applicants have created accounts during a private prelaunch preview. Nineteen have saved partial drafts; none has a final submitted status. Twelve of those nineteen include an uploaded file, and seven contain text only. The preview was intended to let people prepare, not submit early. A migration must not silently turn a draft into a final application or lose a file association. The current main-form draft store can remain read-only while a temporary service operates. Applicants should see what is preserved, what they must review and how to avoid creating duplicate submissions. The draft count is small enough for a supervised reconciliation, but that work has not been performed.

Operations has reserved two trained staff from 15:30 to 16:15 today to prepare a draft inventory and applicant instructions using only reference numbers. Staff can identify saved work and preserve a tenant snapshot. They may not copy personal evidence into a spreadsheet or email it to the testing group. Mira approved the protected preservation method this morning. Ruth can verify completion by 16:15 if no unexpected mismatch appears; this is an assigned checkpoint, not a completed preservation receipt. If the team finds a missing association, the launch decision must include that actual state and an applicant-controlled correction process.

For temporary intake, the Trust can staff two people daily 08:00–18:00 through November 30 and an on-call manager for serious outages. Based on a rehearsal, those staff can handle roughly twenty assisted contacts per day while doing ordinary reconciliation. They cannot manually enter 600 applications in a weekend or promise immediate appointments to everyone. Simple Intake's independent flow is therefore different from a plan that requires all disabled applicants to telephone. A contact queue can supplement the service, but capacity and contact hours must be disclosed. No applicant should be told an application has been accepted solely because a call was logged.

A failed submission can be reviewed using the reference, timestamp and tenant event, with a no-penalty handling instruction if the failure is attributable to the service. Ruth can preserve evidence and recommend a submission-window adjustment to Ada. Only Ada can approve a general deadline extension after funder communication. The proposed November 30 review leaves several days before the current December 4 close, but that alone does not ensure a fair window if significant defects persist. The review must consider actual reach, delays and complaints, including people who could not contact support during working hours.

Ruth favors a usable opening if the approved conditions are met, because a blanket delay also harms people who need time to gather evidence. She is prepared to defer if they are not. Her preference is not written acceptance. At 13:45 she has not seen the failed-notification retest or a preservation receipt and has not instructed the opening job to run. The operational record should retain those separate facts instead of interpreting her willingness to consider a temporary service as consent to whatever engineering eventually delivers.
