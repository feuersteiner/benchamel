# E05 — Current release and accessibility incident policy

Record version: REL-5.1. Owner: product operations director Anya Cole. Effective 2026-11-01. Applies to new public transactional launches and critical user barriers.

A reproduced critical barrier in the required production process changes the launch record to blocked pending an authorized disposition. The blocker must identify the affected task, environment, practical user impact and any safe interim service. Blocked does not mean all static pages must be taken offline. Staff should distinguish a product deployment from a customer's public opening: an engineering release does not itself authorize the customer's intake launch. Account executives and support associates cannot override a critical-barrier flag to protect a sales milestone.

A temporary alternative requires a named operations owner, documented end-to-end test evidence, data handling review and customer operational acceptance. The product operations director may approve use within a specific customer and period. The record must state what is substituted, how applicants discover it, what happens if it fails, and when it is reviewed. Customer agreement to a limited temporary service does not certify the primary product or amend commercial terms. Material scope changes, new data destinations, extra populations or a different vendor require the stated reviewers to revisit the approval. Absence of a reply is not customer acceptance.

Support duty leads may immediately stop a scheduled application-opening job when a newly reproduced barrier presents foreseeable harm. The lead must notify the launch owner, preserve information access and record the state. This short protective stop lasts up to forty-eight hours, allowing a signatory decision rather than an indefinite support veto. Extending beyond that period requires product operations confirmation. A duty lead may prepare customer options and coordinate tests but cannot announce final acceptance, publish an invented completion date or withdraw the customer's public notices without its designated owner.

Engineering fixes require regression coverage for successful and unsuccessful submissions, including error recovery, duplicate attempts, upload limits, declaration and receipt. Passing only the originally reported interaction is insufficient for a release recommendation. A provisional test result may support a scoped operational decision; its limitations must travel with it. A scanner result cannot be called a complete assessment. Release notes must identify remaining known barriers, and old claims cannot remain approved simply because an engineering ticket is open.

Public claims of accessibility, certification or conformance must be supported by an assessment with a matching scope and date. On discovery of a material contradictory barrier, the claims owner must contain and correct the claim through the publishing process. Preserve the earlier statement and recipients for a proportionate correction; deleting a webpage alone does not inform a customer who relied on a sales attachment. Legal review handles actual interpretive disputes, while factual limitations can be disclosed by their operational owners. A customer complaint must not be conditioned on purchase, publicity consent or silence. This policy replaces REL-4.2; the older checklist remains archived for explaining prior workflow, not current launch authority.
