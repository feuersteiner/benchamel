# E04 — Signed accessibility schedule and acceptance boundary

Record version: ACC-SCH-2.0. Attached to September 14 order. Signed by Ada Fen and Lena Orr. Effective 2026-09-14. Maintained by LanternPath contracts operations; file annotation updated 2026-11-23 11:30 UTC.

The schedule requires the delivered applicant workflow to be evaluated against WCAG 2.2 Level AA and to permit applicants to complete the agreed core process using supported keyboard and screen-reader combinations. The supplier will provide a dated accessibility assessment identifying scope, method, known exceptions and remediation status. The schedule does not say that an automated scan is certification. It contains no representation that a government body, W3C or a standards organization has certified LanternPath. No separate certification document is in the contract file. An accessibility statement is a communication record and does not replace the agreed assessment.

Acceptance requires a documented walkthrough of the agreed core process, a defect list and Ruth's written acceptance for the Trust. Acceptance may be withheld for a critical blocker. A critical blocker means a confirmed failure preventing an intended applicant from completing a required step without disproportionate or unsafe assistance, where no approved practical alternative is operating. This is a contractual severity definition used by these fictional parties. It is not a claim about statutory thresholds. The schedule permits a temporary alternative by written operational agreement that identifies scope, limitations, owners, review date and treatment of already saved work. Such an agreement does not assert that the primary workflow conforms or erase open defects.

A temporary service may preserve the planned opening date if the relevant launch authority agrees. An operations acceptance can cover a substitute intake process without accepting the defective module as complete. Any waiver of contractual rights, fee change or permanent reduction of requirements needs the two commercial signatories. The distinction was included so that practical service protection would not force a rushed commercial settlement. Ruth may approve operations under E07; she cannot sign a blanket release or accept unknown future defects. Support should explain those boundaries in ordinary language if it proposes a temporary process.

The vendor must give a named owner and a remediation plan when a critical blocker is reproduced. It may describe an engineering target as a target. A completion date becomes a customer commitment only when the engineering owner confirms the delivery, test and release dependencies. A failed test must be communicated and cannot be hidden behind a release label. The customer is expected to provide representative synthetic scenarios and promptly decide proposed operational choices, but it is not responsible for designing the vendor's upload widget or proving a disability.

The schedule allows escalation to a joint review if acceptance or scope is disputed. It does not specify an automatic refund, damages award or cancellation on a first complaint. The main agreement contains ordinary negotiated remedies, none invoked at this cutoff. Contracts operations has not determined whether the August sales correspondence creates additional obligations. The signed schedule remains usable evidence while that question is reviewed; an unresolved legal interpretation is not a reason to withhold a practical incident update or continue a misleading marketing claim.
