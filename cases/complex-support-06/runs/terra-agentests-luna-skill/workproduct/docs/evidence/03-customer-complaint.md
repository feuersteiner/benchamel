# E03 — Applicant liaison complaint and requested response

Record version: TICKET-614.3. Authenticated Trust thread. Original complaint 2026-11-20 14:10 UTC; latest message 2026-11-23 11:35 UTC. Owner: Dev Shah, applicant liaison.

Dev reports that a volunteer tester using keyboard navigation and a screen reader could enter eligibility answers but could not independently complete the evidence-and-declaration stage. Focus moved behind a dialog after an upload error, the error was not announced, and the final declaration control could not reliably be found. Another volunteer using a keyboard without a screen reader became stuck in the evidence picker. Dev says these failures affect a task people must complete to apply; describing them as cosmetic defects would not answer the complaint. The volunteers used synthetic application material and were not applicants seeking preferential treatment.

On November 20 Dev asked for an accessible independent way to submit, the scope of the supplier's prior accessibility claims, a named technical owner and a realistic update before the board pack. He did not ask for medical proof, a legal conclusion or a universal guarantee. The initial response suggested that a family member could help and attached a generic checklist. Dev replied that assistance may expose income and employment evidence, that not everyone has a trusted helper, and that an application route should not require applicants to reveal a disability to obtain ordinary service. That is a practical privacy and dignity concern, not an allegation that every assisted service is necessarily unacceptable.

The latest message confirms the Trust will not collect applicant passwords for staff entry. Dev can explain a voluntary assisted route if it preserves applicant control and is one option among usable choices. He cannot agree that the same failed widget is suitable merely because a support agent can operate it with a mouse. An instructional video would not let the tester tab to an unreachable control. Asking the tester to try a different browser without identifying the known defect would consume time and could imply the report is not credible.

Dev has already provided the browser and screen-reader versions in E08. He will join a short session today at 15:40 with Ruth and one volunteer if invited before 15:15. The volunteer can decline without affecting the complaint. Testing must use fabricated evidence files; a recording requires specific optional permission and must not capture unrelated desktop content. Dev wants a written alternative if no session occurs. Support should not make progress depend on the availability of this particular volunteer, who is not the vendor's unpaid quality team.

The Trust asks that any response distinguish the immediate intake decision from the longer repair and conformance program. It wants to know whether information pages can publish, whether applications can actually open, and whether notice can be corrected before 18:00. The request remains live even if a service credit is later considered. Dev's frustration concerns exclusion, wasted testing time and trust in procurement statements. Neither the complaint nor the polite signoff grants launch consent, a case closure, a release, permission to quote the tester publicly or agreement to a more expensive plan.
