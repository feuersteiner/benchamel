# E08 — Security working assessment and preservation receipt

Record version: SEC-482.3. Owner: Security analyst Priya Sen. Snapshot: 2024-03-01 13:05 UTC. Status: preliminary assessment, not final incident attribution. Preservation receipt P-31 is complete.

Billing counters show approximately 190 terabytes of account transfer across the incident window. Security retained an edge-request sample covering roughly seven percent of the relevant interval, concentrated in two geographic regions and excluding some cache delivery paths. The sample contains repeated requests for a large Linden Festival asset, ordinary browser-like user agents, cloud-hosted network origins, and a mixture of successful and interrupted responses. The data supports abnormal concentration. It does not let an analyst reconstruct every byte or distinguish all automated clients from legitimate viewers.

The working hypotheses include direct embedding of the large file, a scraper retry pattern, campaign interest amplified by caching behavior, an intentional nuisance flood, and combinations of those mechanisms. Browser-like user agents do not prove that humans made the requests. A cloud-hosted source does not identify a person, prove malicious intent, or prove that the customer ordered a test. The observed repetition is also compatible with third-party retry logic. Staff should avoid selecting the most dramatic explanation merely because the customer initially used the word attack.

The aggregate transfer counter and sampled requests have different scopes. The analyst has not found evidence that the counter was fabricated, but has not independently certified the entire billing pipeline. One preliminary chart used request count where a colleague expected transfer volume; that chart is withdrawn from external use until its units are corrected. A corrected chart may help explain concentration without establishing responsibility. The investigation should compare time windows and units before presenting numerical conclusions to the customer.

P-31 confirms that the available sample, query definitions, counter export, and relevant configuration snapshots were preserved at 12:55 UTC. A data minimization copy can be shared with Aster after third-party identifiers are removed. Raw visitor addresses across all downstream clients should not be emailed to the agency administrator merely because the account contains those sites. Security and Privacy will determine the safe scope of disclosure. The receipt satisfies the preservation condition in A-73; it does not promise complete historical logs that were never retained.

The next security update is due March 4 at 16:00 UTC. That update may narrow hypotheses or explain limitations; it is not a promise to identify an attacker by then. Priya can answer a short question before 16:30 today if the support owner needs to describe the sample accurately. The billing decision may proceed under the director's commercial authority while attribution remains open, provided the response keeps those separate.

No current evidence indicates a material threat to shared infrastructure. The security emergency exception for unilateral containment is therefore not triggered at this snapshot. Ongoing Linden asset traffic still creates financial exposure, so targeted mitigation is appropriate under the customer's recorded consent. If a new infrastructure threat appears, Security must record the changed evidence, scope of emergency action, and customer update. A later emergency cannot be invented to justify an earlier blanket shutdown.
