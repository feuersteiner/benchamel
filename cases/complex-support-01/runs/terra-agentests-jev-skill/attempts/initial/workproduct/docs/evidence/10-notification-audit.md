# E10 — Notification and review audit

Record version: OPS-771.1. Owner: Messaging Operations. Snapshot: 2024-03-01 10:30 UTC. Status: partial audit with unresolved delivery and usefulness questions. Times use UTC.

On February 15 at 22:12 the usage service created a threshold notification for BILLING-A. The mail gateway accepted the job and later recorded delivery to the receiving server. The record contains no human-open confirmation and no copy of the recipient's inbox. A receiving-server success supports a delivery event at that boundary. It does not prove that Mara or another authorized person read the notice, understood projected cost, or had a practical opportunity to stop the spike. The customer reports no useful warning was seen.

The notice used the subject “Additional transfer package added” and listed an increment of usage rather than the projected account total. The template did not state the accumulating six-figure exposure. Whether that notice met the signed schedule's general notification language is a legal and commercial review question, not something the support agent should settle by saying the customer ignored an obvious bill. There is no evidence that Aster intentionally disabled the notification setting. The address configuration still matches the executed account record.

A second job on February 17 was deferred repeatedly by the receiving server and expired from the queue. A retry summary in an internal dashboard counted job creation as notification success. Messaging Operations considers that dashboard label misleading for this use. Staff cannot claim two successful notices from it. The first delivery record and the second failed job should both be preserved so that the investigation does not erase inconvenient evidence in either direction.

The exceptional-invoice review flag should have opened a finance task before the February 24 invoice was released. The flag was present, but the queue assignment rule pointed to an inactive team. This is a confirmed internal process failure. It does not explain the traffic itself. Finance's remedy approval relies partly on this failure without requiring proof that every mail delivery also failed. A customer-facing acknowledgment can be clear about the review miss while preserving the uncertainty over individual notice receipt.

The corrected queue route is now active for newly generated invoices. The old invoice and notices remain in the audit trail. The change is not evidence that all future billing risk is eliminated; threshold notifications still describe usage rather than enforce spending. A short-term human review has been assigned for Aster through March 5. That assignment needs an owner and escalation path if the available reviewer is absent; a vague assurance that the system is fixed would overstate what changed.

Messaging Operations requests that support avoid asking Mara to resend mailbox screenshots containing client information. A useful narrow question is whether the role mailbox was monitored during the event, but the answer is not a condition of the approved relief. The current evidence already supports owning the review failure. If Mara later supplies notice details voluntarily, record them as additional evidence and explain any safe redaction request before transferring them across departments.
