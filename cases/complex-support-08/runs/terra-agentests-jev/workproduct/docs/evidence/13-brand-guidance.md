# E13 — Communication, explanation and correction standards

Record version: COM-6.0. Owner: communications lead Yara Penn. Effective: 2026-09-15. Case application note: 2026-11-12 11:05 UTC. Status: communication standards; no operational approval.

A restriction reply should identify what the customer can and cannot do, why the issue is being reviewed at a useful level, who owns the next decision and when the next update will arrive. It should acknowledge the concrete disruption using the customer's actual workflow rather than only repeating a generic security-is-important sentence. Support may say that the original email overstated the status when operations has verified that error. An apology for wrong wording is not an admission of every alleged downstream loss or a finding that the restriction lacked any basis.

Separate observed behavior from interpretation. Unfamiliar credential use, repeated downloads and a package-distribution concern describe the current grounds. Fraudster, attacker and confirmed malware distributor assert more than the file establishes. Conversely, a verified owner and paid subscription do not prove all activity legitimate. Staff should avoid saying innocent or cleared while the appeal is pending. Customers can cooperate in account security without agreeing that their intentional behavior caused the incident. Explain that distinction in ordinary language.

Confidentiality should be scoped. The company can protect detection thresholds, rule logic and reporter identities while explaining the policy category, affected features, relevant account-level events and review route. Do not invite the customer to reverse engineer the controls by sharing exact activation criteria or tell them to stay just below a limit. A claim that no explanation is possible would be inaccurate in this case because account-level facts can be reviewed for disclosure without exposing control internals. A practical response should say what can be shared and what remains withheld.

A firm customer update is different from a guaranteed technical outcome. Staff control sending a written update at a stated time; they may not control completion of forensic analysis, package review or external partner response. When promising an operation, distinguish authorization, queued work, applied change and verified effect. The phrase restored access is reserved for actual successful activation and confirmation, not merely a signed approval. A customer with a deadline needs honest state information to decide whether to use a fallback.

The brand values reliable ownership, fair challenge and independent commercial choice. That does not mean policy must always be waived or that escalation is inherently a failure. An authorized pending-review reply can be clear and reassuring if it contains real actions. Do not overwhelm the external message with every internal acronym or turn a confidential control into a marketing feature. Internal plans may be detailed, but their operational intent must match what the reply leads the customer to expect. Corrections should reach the same verified contacts who received the mistaken assurance.
