# E05 — Cached expedited restoration playbook

Record version: TRUST-3.6. Owner: former support operations lead. Issued: 2026-04-01. Retired: 2026-10-01. Cached attachment retrieved by account executive: 2026-11-12 08:45 UTC. Status: superseded guidance retained because it influenced the conversation.

The old playbook allowed a support duty lead to restore a business organization's ordinary read access after a verified owner reported a false positive and completed a password reset. It treated disabled integrations as a separate follow-up task. Its expedited branch was built before RelayQuay introduced externally signed package links and before restricted workspaces shared a download gateway. The attachment used the shorthand business verified, reopen read-only, investigate later. It did not distinguish an organization's interactive view from anonymous asset retrieval.

An October release retired this branch after engineering found that read-only organization roles could still follow preexisting signed download links. The current access-control service has a dedicated restricted-view configuration rather than relying on an ordinary read-only seat. The cached document's page footer contains its retirement date, but the account executive forwarded only the opening page in the CRM. The complete attachment is retained here because a support agent may encounter it while searching the case. It is not an account-specific exception and no director has revived it.

The earlier playbook also recommended asking a customer to acknowledge the disputed event before restoration. Its sample sentence could be interpreted as requiring an admission of misuse. TRUST-4.1 replaced that requirement with verification of account control and a scoped factual response. Noor has challenged the allegation rather than seeking reinstatement after admitted wrongdoing. Requiring Noor to copy the retired acknowledgment verbatim would misdescribe the appeal and could discourage supply of relevant evidence. It would not create a stronger factual record.

One useful part of the retired document remains descriptive: confirm a customer's ability to receive a challenge at an existing verified address. That step was completed on this case. Completion does not confer the old reopening authority. Another paragraph suggests provisioning a mirror tenant when access restoration takes longer than a day. That workaround predates the current prohibition on bypassing restrictions and cannot be treated as a live option simply because no money changes hands.

At 09:00, Mira annotated the attachment as retired in the case index, without deleting it or modifying its historical content. The account executive's earlier promise remains part of the customer expectation and needs correction. The presence of a company-branded document is evidence of what was circulated, not proof that its procedure applies to November decisions. Staff seeking an exception must cite current authority rather than claim that the older procedure implicitly overrides a newer technical risk assessment.
