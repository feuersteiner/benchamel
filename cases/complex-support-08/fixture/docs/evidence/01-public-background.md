S01: GitHub's Appeal and Reinstatement documentation distinguishes disputing an enforcement decision with additional information from seeking access after corrective changes. It describes staff review, human appeal decisions, and independent review when the original decision maker would otherwise reject their own decision. This is an attributed description of the current public process, not evidence that GitHub reviewed the fictional account or guarantees any particular result.

S02: GitHub's Active Malware or Exploits policy distinguishes direct support for unlawful technical attacks from dual-use security material. It describes rare restrictions on widely abused dual-use content, temporary restrictions where feasible, contact with owners where possible, and access to an appeals process. Legitimate purpose and potential harmful use can therefore coexist in a published policy; neither establishes the fictional customer's conduct.

S03: GitHub's Preventing unauthorized access documentation describes password changes, review of keys and applications, protection of the associated email account, and contacting support if restrictions remain. It states that suspicious activity may lead to restrictions or security suspension. Those public instructions ground the distinction between securing an account and resolving the policy basis for a restriction; they are not a forensic report about this case.

S04: Discord's Warning System documentation, marked updated June 5, 2026, describes notices identifying policy, actions, and sometimes content details. It distinguishes restrictions from the continuing effect of a violation and describes review requests when a user believes a mistake occurred. This source supplies an example of explanation and review design, not a general promise that every enforcement system exposes its detection details.

S05: Discord's How You Can Appeal Our Actions page, dated February 16, 2024, acknowledges possible mistakes and describes review of a specific violation. It states that removing an erroneous violation can remove resulting actions while other active violations can still affect access. The attributed point is the separability of review outcomes and remaining restrictions, not a claim that the fictional service follows Discord's implementation.
