# E05 — Current launch control and operational acceptance policy

Record version: DEL-4.3. Owner: RelayGrid Delivery Assurance. Effective: 2025-05-01. Case applicability annotation: 2025-06-12 12:20 UTC. Status: current internal operating policy, communicated to implementation staff May 1; project-specific references reflect the later applicability annotation.

A launch status describes a bounded configuration, workload and time. Staff must not use ready without naming what has passed, what remains open and who can authorize the switch. Production tenant availability, a successful demonstration and signed customer acceptance are separate events. The policy requires a current defect register, tested recovery procedure, customer operations owner, monitoring owner and explicit go/no-go record before primary commercial processing begins. Contract-specific acceptance terms remain applicable; an internal status cannot remove a signed deliverable.

Severity one means observed or reasonably foreseeable corruption, duplication or loss of a commercial transaction without a proven containment mechanism. It blocks unattended production processing. Severity two means a material workflow defect with a verified bounded workaround and auditable recovery. A downgrade needs Delivery Assurance approval based on test evidence, not a salesperson's preference or a quieter dashboard. Synthetic staging can proceed under a test permit if isolated from commercial events. Commercial pilots require a scoped operational exception under the current authority matrix and customer approval. An exception does not declare the full implementation complete.

A launch request must identify the event path, data snapshot, software versions, volume limits, excluded operations and rollback trigger. After a failure, staff must compare source intent, durable event receipt, downstream commit and reconciliation rather than infer business success from a transport acknowledgment. Retries may reapply a business effect if suppression is incomplete. Replaying an entire batch against live systems requires an approved reconciliation plan because an earlier apparent failure may have committed downstream. Duplicate reservations can block inventory even when no duplicate invoice is issued.

Support leads may schedule reviews, preserve case records, correct misleading customer statements and keep milestone invoicing blocked where acceptance is absent. They cannot accept business risk for the customer, waive contract criteria, authorize partner spend or guarantee another company's response. They must provide a bounded update when a requested outcome is not yet known. A missed specialist checkpoint requires a timely status update, escalation to the duty delivery director and a revised controlled commitment. Silence until a full diagnosis is available is not an approved process.

The policy provides no bonus for launching by an advertised date. Where launch incentives exist, the decision record must retain failed tests and unresolved responsibility claims. A customer may reasonably prefer a slower complete cutover or a limited usable process, but customer urgency is not consent to undisclosed risk. A customer signature procured by describing an untested workflow as reliable does not cure the underlying disclosure problem. Archived DEL-3.1 in E06 is retained because a project ticket still links to it. Its simplified project closure fields must not be used as current authority to trigger final billing or declare the implementation accepted.
