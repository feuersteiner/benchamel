# E13 — Customer communication and launch claims guidance

Record version: COM-6.2. Owner: RelayGrid Communications. Effective: 2025-05-15. Status: current general brand guidance for delivery escalations.

Customer language should connect the observed failure to the customer's practical decision. Acknowledge the impact of unreliable operations and explain what the vendor owns. Do not make the customer translate internal queue names or mediate a dispute between teams before receiving help. One support owner can coordinate work without pretending to control every supplier. Naming technical dependencies is useful when it explains a limit or needed action; listing every organization to diffuse responsibility is not. The external reply should make the next owner and update time easy to find.

Do not describe a production tenant as a completed implementation unless the relevant workflow and acceptance states support it. When earlier language created an incorrect impression, correct it directly. An apology need not concede every legal claim, and avoidance of an absolute liability conclusion need not make the reply evasive. Distinguish responsibility for a reproduced connector defect from allocation of all project delay. Facts can be acknowledged before an exhaustive root-cause or damages review. Customer allegations should be attributed fairly rather than repeated as proven technical findings.

Use ordinary operational terms. Received means the gateway accepted a message; reserved means the warehouse recorded inventory; invoiced means the accounting service created a document. If those distinctions matter to the proposed course, explain them briefly. Do not overwhelm the board briefing with implementation jargon or paste raw logs. A limited workload must be described as limited. Pilot controls should not be represented as proof the agreed full workflow is ready. Words such as guaranteed, fully resolved and seamless require evidence and appropriate authority.

Sales support may assist in resolving a customer problem, but basic delivery correction must not be sold again under an emergency premium plan. Optional service may be described accurately when its actual limits are relevant, with the customer free to decline. A quote expiry must not be presented as a condition for honoring an existing contract or a previously approved reimbursement. Do not request positive publicity, silence, a reference call or an acceptance certificate in exchange for corrective work. The customer's public criticism does not change technical truth or financial authority.

Every written update should distinguish decisions taken, actions proposed and matters awaiting approval. An update deadline is a commitment the support team can control; a repair completion time may depend on unverified tests. If no new result arrives, the owner still explains the current state, what blocked progress and the next decision time. Brand review does not require hiding failures to protect a launch narrative. This policy does not prescribe which operational route Vela should choose; it governs the honesty and usefulness of the communication supporting that choice. Specific confidential partner pricing and personal data remain subject to the disclosure controls, even when transparency is appropriate.
