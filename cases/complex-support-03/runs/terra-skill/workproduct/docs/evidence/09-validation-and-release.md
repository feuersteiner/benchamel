A customer export requires checking the specific tender evidence set, not extrapolating from a random sample to a claim of complete recovery.

The 74 accepted changes must be reconciled by identity and dependency. For the 21 revision pointers, a target object and its prior revision must be identified. For each of the 31 approval transitions, the record must show the approving role, intended document revision and event ordering. Comments may be useful context but cannot substitute for an approval. Noor can validate structural links; Meridian must determine whether a restored or manually recreated signoff is substantively valid for its tender. Neither role can manufacture the other's evidence.

Outgoing connector queues remain paused. The queue snapshot contains 86 undelivered notifications and 12 previously delivered items whose acknowledgments are incomplete. Bulk replay would risk duplicate notices and could expose a restored older revision in the customer dashboard. The customer has authorized continued preservation, not delivery. A technical recovery does not implicitly authorize notifying external reviewers. A release plan must identify the queues that remain paused and how the customer will approve any later replay.

Read-only extraction avoids production replacement but still needs access controls. The export specialist must bind the package to Meridian's existing administrator identities, include hashes for included files, list exclusions, distinguish restored records from locally supplied evidence, and disable live approval links. An offline pack can be useful without representing the entire current workspace. A package containing cross-tenant storage diagnostics, key material or internal raw logs is not an acceptable substitute for a customer-specific record. The data team has enough information to build a minimized discrepancy report without those items.
