# E18 — Supplier work order and cost classification dispute

Record version: MW-COST.2. Owner: Meridian commercial owner Sera Bell. Supplied: 2025-05-14 11:30 UTC. Status: authenticated supplier documents and customer explanation; claim approval pending.

Meridian signed a $18,000 work order with Vale Document Control yesterday to help assemble the bid. The scope includes routine tender formatting, document collection, upload preparation and responding to recovery gaps if the platform fails. The supplier has not split the fee by activity. Sera describes the whole amount as recovery expense because the team is now occupied by the outage. The contract was committed before the incident, and Finance has not determined how much additional reconstruction work it represents. The document establishes a real commercial commitment in this fictional setting, not automatic eligibility for the entire allowance or loss claim.

At 11:10 a second supplier, Reed Recovery Lab, sent a $2,750 fixed-price work order for a diagnostic reconstruction session today. The described deliverables are comparison of Meridian's local export against the receipt-derived discrepancy list, identification of missing revision relationships and a written recovery map. It expressly excludes tender formatting, claim valuation, legal advice and restoration guarantees. The full charge becomes nonrefundable when a specialist is reserved; delivery of the written map is included even if no records can be recovered. If not reserved by 12:20, the specialist may be assigned elsewhere.

Sera signed this second work order at 11:22 to keep the option open, with a customer cancellation right until the reservation is accepted. Reed has not accepted it or invoiced the charge. Sera asks Flint Ledger to confirm payment so she can let the reservation become binding. Her signature authenticates the proposed supplier scope, but is not a Flint Ledger purchasing approval. The work order does not yet show an actually incurred charge. Sera says she will bear the charge if necessary and separately pursue reimbursement; she has not authorized support to tell Reed that Flint Ledger guarantees all fees.

Reed would work only on Meridian's supplied files and minimized customer discrepancy list. Sera has designated the named supplier organization for that purpose, but no supplier staff account has been verified for direct portal access. Owen can retrieve the package and transfer it using Meridian's approved channel. No raw internal logs, secret configuration files or cross-tenant records are required. Cora has not signed a new direct-access arrangement. The supplier does not need access to the production database to perform the stated comparison.

The commercial deadline precedes Nessa's expected 13:15 response and falls before her 12:30 slot. A rapid classification question might obtain an earlier answer, but no such answer exists at the cutoff. Dev reads the signed work order as potentially eligible fixed-price reconstruction; another finance analyst reads the initial nonrefundable charge as reservation expense until work starts. Both agree that $2,750 is below $3,000 and that amount alone cannot settle the scope question. No one has approved a $18,000 payment or a $120,000 guarantee.
