# E05 — Current recovery control policy

Record version: REC-4.1. Owner: reliability director Anika Shaw. Effective: 2025-04-01. Status: current mandatory operating control for customer data repair; fictional policy.

A restore is a controlled data change, not a ticket status. Operators must preserve the damaged state and any accepted post-snapshot writes before repair. A recovered database must be restored first into an isolated environment with outbound connectors disabled. The operator records source snapshot, application version, encryption-key references, table and object counts, relation checks and the known recovery-point gap. A successful backup job or database startup alone is not evidence that the application can reconstruct a coherent customer workspace.

The incident commander can approve non-destructive isolated restoration tests and prepare a read-only extract for the existing authorized account administrators. Test costs and customer access must remain within the incident and privacy controls. No production tenant replacement is permitted until the technical owner records validation and the commercial account owner receives a plain explanation of lost or uncertain changes, downtime, identifier effects and rollback limits. The authorized customer must explicitly consent to the described cutover. A customer's request to recover data is not consent to silently discard accepted later writes.

Recovery tests must use a copy. Operators may not delete surviving media, expire incident journals, rewrite source backups or resume automated queues just to make a test pass. When replaying journals, compare record identity and application semantics; repeated approval transitions and invitation deliveries are not harmless duplicates. Separate technically replayable events from events requiring customer verification. Original timestamps may be preserved where supported, but staff may not invent an approval signature or backdate a manually recreated decision to make a package appear complete.

Recovery-point and recovery-time objectives are planning measures, not promises for every loss mechanism. Contractual service terms may include separate obligations and remedies. Staff must distinguish an estimate of copy completion from validation, customer review and release to production. If a published estimate changes, communicate what has changed and the next controllable checkpoint. Final root cause is not a prerequisite to sending a useful interim status. When no completion estimate is sufficiently supported, state the uncertainty and identify the work that could narrow it.

The customer incident owner must coordinate engineering, support and commercial records, including deadlines outside the service. A credit does not close a recovery or information request. Work on mitigation and evidence preservation continues while liability or compensation is reviewed. Recovery priority is based on operational impact and safe resource use; a new purchase, renewal or public silence must not be a prerequisite for ordinary incident recovery. An optional paid feature may be described for future use only with accurate scope and without implying it can retroactively create missing data.

This policy supersedes REC-2.6 for tenant-level data repair after April 1. Historical playbooks may supply technical clues, but their shortcuts do not grant present execution authority. The approval register records case-specific spending and customer permissions separately from this control policy.
