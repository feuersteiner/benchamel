# E06 — Archived fast-recovery playbook and failed drill

Record version: REC-2.6. Owner: platform operations archive. Issued: 2024-08-19. Superseded: 2025-04-01 by REC-4.1. Archive annotation recorded: 2025-05-14 08:05 UTC. Status: historical training material; not current cutover authority.

The archived playbook describes replacing a single-workspace database from the newest completed snapshot, restarting the web service and asking the customer to report discrepancies. Its triage card used a two-hour target from snapshot selection to web availability. That number came from a small test tenant without attachment history, customer connectors or recent accepted writes. It did not cover a real tender evidence bundle, relation reconstruction or human approval review. The old card was still present in the support macro library this morning and appears to be the source of the 07:24 support promise.

The old procedure advised temporarily disabling browser edits but allowed background synchronizers to reconnect as the service restarted. In a March 12 drill, the browser looked healthy while a restarted connector delivered duplicate approval requests and replaced two newer document pointers with old values. The drill found no issue with raw object availability, yet the approval register could not be reconciled automatically. A green web-health check therefore measured a narrower property than recovery of business records. The drill did not involve Meridian or any customer data.

A second problem was identifier handling. Test restores generated new workspace and document identifiers while some exports retained original identifiers. Downstream systems then treated recovered records as new documents. Staff manually mapped a small sample but did not prove the mapping for a tenant of Meridian's size. REC-4.1 now requires documenting these relationships and disables outbound effects during testing. The archive can still help engineers find the location of snapshot manifests; it cannot authorize ignoring those newer controls.

The training slide also suggested a standard $500 service credit as an immediate apology. That was the support limit for fee adjustments and never authorized consequential-loss compensation. A handwritten presenter note saying managers can sort out the rest referred to escalation, not an unlimited delegated budget. There is no evidence that the customer saw or accepted this internal playbook. It cannot reduce obligations in the signed order or bind the customer to an older recovery point.

At 08:05 the reliability director removed this playbook from active incident links and asked Support Operations to disable the two-hour macro. The archive copy is retained because it helps explain the premature promise and system history. No corrected macro has yet been sent to Meridian. Reviewing this artifact should not distract engineering from the current chain and relation checks. It identifies where an outdated estimate came from, rather than proving that all existing backups are unusable or that a two-hour repair can never be safe for a different incident.
