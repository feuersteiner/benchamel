# E13 — Brand, correction and trust communication guidance

Record version: COM-6.1. Owner: communications director Rina Sol. Effective: 2025-01-10. Incident application note: 2025-05-14 09:15 UTC. Status: current communication standard.

Northline's brand promise is practical control with understandable security. In an incident, trust depends on accurate scope and accountable next actions. Staff should write in plain language and identify which service or data category a statement concerns. Terms such as safe, unaffected, secure and contained require a defined subject and evidence. Reassurance can describe verified protections and competent ownership; it must not imply complete knowledge where investigation is still open. Technical qualifiers must explain a decision-relevant limit rather than bury it in a footnote after a categorical headline.

A correction should identify the previous statement, explain what the current record supports and apologize for the confusion or error Northline caused. Do not blame the customer for taking an official message at face value. Do not attribute fault to an individual employee when the customer's need is an accurate account position. A short acknowledgment of the process failure can be sufficient. Avoid lengthy internal routing history that distracts from known exposure, protection and time of the next update. The customer can receive a useful account statement while the company continues a separate personnel or process review.

Account-level customer communication differs from public incident publication. The public status page reports availability and currently shows production operating normally. It is not an exposure register and its green state does not prove no data access. A public advisory requires the incident command and Legal publication process. Support should not invent a public acknowledgment date or promise that Alder can control notification to other customers. Conversely, absence of a public advisory is not a reason to deny Alder its verified account facts. Messages to customers should be consistent in scope, while protecting confidential differences among accounts.

Commercial language must not trivialize a security concern as a renewal obstacle. A board deciding whether to entrust identity infrastructure is entitled to ask hard questions. Northline may describe the continuity value of an extension or renewal without implying that a purchase solves forensic uncertainty. Optional controls should be presented with actual limits and operational tradeoffs. If a plan recommends wider rotation, explain interruption risk and obtain the appropriate operational consent. Neither fear nor euphemism is a suitable sales technique.

Do not promise the customer absolute safety, all future incidents will be prevented, or a guaranteed final report before a deadline not approved by the investigation owner. Give the exact next update time and who owns it. If facts remain unchanged at that time, say so and explain the dependency. A respectful message can still be inadequate if it offers no usable information. A technically accurate message can still be harmful if it hides a known support-data retrieval behind a general statement that production remains available. Communication quality must be judged in the context of the actions actually planned.
