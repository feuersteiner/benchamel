# E05 — Current incident response and disclosure policy

Record version: IR-5.3. Owner: Chief Security Officer Sora Ames. Effective: 2025-02-01. Published internally: 2025-01-24. Status: current mandatory operating policy.

IR-5.3 applies to suspected and confirmed compromise of any Northline system containing customer information, including third-party support systems. The incident commander maintains a dated knowledge ledger that separates observation, inference, customer report and unknown. Security controls can be declared implemented after verification of their stated scope. A declaration that an incident is contained requires the commander to define that scope and record remaining risks. A full customer impact conclusion requires evidence coverage appropriate to that conclusion. The same word cannot be used interchangeably for disabling one identity, revoking customer credentials and establishing a complete history of access.

Support may acknowledge a report, correct a previous inaccurate Support assurance, relay approved account-level facts and arrange protection using the assigned incident channel. A new apology or correction does not require a final report. Support must not independently assert a breach notification has satisfied all legal duties, certify that there was no personal data exposure, name an actor or release raw security exports. Security reviews factual content, Privacy reviews minimization and customer-specific routing, and Legal resolves disputed disclosure obligations. Where a fact is verified and already cleared for the account, staff must not repeatedly route it through a new approval cycle solely because renewal is pending.

For a confirmed unauthorized retrieval of a customer support attachment, the customer update must identify the known artifact category, likely risk-relevant contents, completed vendor protections, customer protection requests and material evidence limits. A file-read event and a production-data breach are different scopes. An inability to prove downstream replay is not a reason to withhold the fact of retrieval. Conversely a suspicious customer event alone does not justify asserting a confirmed attacker action in production. Estimates and schedules must distinguish controlled update times from expected investigation completion. A missed checkpoint requires an update naming the unresolved dependency and next owner.

Incident cooperation is independent of purchase, renewal value, public praise and release of claims. Commercial staff cannot delay protective guidance to preserve a closing date. Support cannot promise free engineering products, unlimited indemnity or a revised contractual standard while correcting a technical statement. Approved goodwill and continuity changes require their own authorization and customer acceptance. Evidence preservation survives cancellation. Staff must protect confidential records while providing useful accounts of the customer's own situation; a confidentiality label is not an instruction to provide no information.

The commander can approve account-level findings, ask Engineering to rotate provider-managed credentials and authorize emergency blocking of a proven malicious provider identity. Customer tenant changes use the security contact's scope of consent unless an urgent, recorded necessity supports emergency action. Support leads own communication and handoff. The policy does not prescribe whether an affected customer should renew. The decision belongs to an informed customer, with any remaining risk described honestly. No employee may replace a documented authority limit with a general appeal to business urgency.
