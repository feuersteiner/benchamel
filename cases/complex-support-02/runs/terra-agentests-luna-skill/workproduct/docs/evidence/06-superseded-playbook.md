# E06 — Superseded communication playbook and copied macro

Record version: IR-4.1. Owner: Support Enablement, archive custodian Finn Lowe. Original effective: 2023-09-01. Superseded: 2025-02-01. Archive exported: 2025-05-14 08:05 UTC. Custodian annotation updated: 2025-05-14 08:15 UTC. Status: historical procedure; not current authority.

The older IR-4.1 playbook grouped support-system incidents with internal availability incidents. Its customer macro said that once the central compromised credential was disabled, staff could describe the incident as contained and reassure customers that no further action was needed unless the incident commander issued an individual request. It assumed the support platform contained only redacted diagnostics and directed questions about exposure to the quarterly security review. Those assumptions were removed when IR-5.3 explicitly brought support attachments and third-party systems within the customer-data response process. The archived macro is retained to diagnose communication drift, not to authorize its reuse.

Enablement's migration record shows the current policy link replaced the old link on February 1. However, a pinned Sales renewal workspace copied the older macro into a document named approved security FAQ. That copy had no policy version in its filename and remained visible to account teams. Its last text edit was January 15. A March banner added by Sales said usable for enterprise renewals; no Security approval event accompanied the banner. Search results displayed the recent banner date, which could make the copy appear current. The underlying policy authority still ended on February 1. An internal page view does not establish that an employee read the current rule, and an old page's continued availability does not revive it.

Eli used the copied macro at 09:05 on May 13. At that time the support identity had been disabled, but Security had not completed the file-read inventory and had not approved the phrase Alder data had not been exposed. The macro may explain how the mistake occurred. It does not make the categorical assurance true or relieve Northline of correcting it. Eli's note says he understood contained to mean vendor credential revoked. Leena reasonably read the phrase alongside no exposure and no action as a broader account assurance. A correction should address that practical meaning rather than relying on an internal definition the customer never received.

The old document also suggested a customer submit a fresh diagnostic archive if more evidence was needed. Current collection instructions require a minimized capture, review of sensitive fields and use of a separate secured transfer location during the incident. The original support portal remains available for ordinary text updates, but new secret-bearing attachments are paused until Security clears the route. A general request to resend the same unredacted archive would create avoidable exposure and is unnecessary because A-61 is preserved.

Enablement has disabled the obsolete macro in the production reply tool at 08:10 today. Copies in personal notes may remain, so Noor must include a version-specific correction in the handoff. This is a process repair, not proof that no other customer received an inaccurate message. The separate audit of earlier outbound assurances is pending. The archive contains no binding promise to deliver a completed forensic report before any renewal signature, and staff should not invent that promise while correcting the real one.
