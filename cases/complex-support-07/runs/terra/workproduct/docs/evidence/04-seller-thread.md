Record version: MSG-S.2. Owner: seller support. Messages: October 16–20, 2026; compiled 08:40 UTC October 20. Status: authenticated account statements, not verified dispatch reconstruction.

Ada reported October 16 at 13:10 that Lumen shipped the tested body ending 641 and that Quay's photographed body was not the one sold. Ada supplied packing photographs, a service bench checklist and an outbound label. One photograph shows a legible serial fragment 641 on a body beside the case; another shows a closed case on the packing table. Neither is a continuous record of closing, labeling and carrier handoff. Ada says a single employee did the packing and cannot explain why a lower-resolution export in the listing history seems to show a 614 fragment. Ada believes that image was an old stock photograph and says the listing's written identifier controlled.

On October 17, Ada asked for the buyer's full identity documents, IP address, previous disputes and studio access record, saying that this information would allow Lumen to prove a switch. Seller support declined unrestricted disclosure and invited a specific question relevant to the goods. Ada considers that refusal unfair because the buyer has seen Lumen's business address. That asymmetry is a real complaint about process; it does not establish a right to private risk data. The marketplace can offer a minimized evidence exchange and an explanation of what was withheld without circulating identity files.

On October 19, Ada described payroll due October 21 and said holding the entire $4,520 payable would require postponing a supplier payment. Lumen asked for $3,000 to be released as the value of undisputed accessories. The invoice does not separately price accessories, and Quay disputes one battery and the working matched-kit representation. Ada supplied a workshop valuation allocating $3,000 to accessories and $1,800 to the body. It was produced after the dispute and was not accepted at purchase. Ada has not provided evidence of an immediate safety or welfare crisis, but the commercial harm of the hold is concrete.

October 20, 08:15, Ada offered two possibilities for discussion: prompt return to a neutral depot with inspection by an independent camera technician, or a platform-funded closure that pays the full original net sale amount and takes any salvage. Ada would not accept a debit justified only by an opaque fraud score, nor a public statement that Lumen had sold a counterfeit or stolen unit. The willingness to discuss options is not itself signed consent to their terms. E18 contains later authenticated choices with a narrower scope.

Lumen's account remains able to list unrelated stock, answer messages and receive payouts for other orders. Ada asks support to remove the dispute from next month's seller badge metrics. The current policy freezes the disputed order's metric pending a decision; it does not promise permanent deletion of an adverse finding. Lumen's paid shop package expires October 24. Ada says renewal depends on fair treatment, but no document gives commercial staff authority to buy favorable adjudication by offering a package discount.
