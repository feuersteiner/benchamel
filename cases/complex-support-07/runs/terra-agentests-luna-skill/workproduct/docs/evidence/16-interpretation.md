# E16 — Trust and counsel note on severability and rights

Record version: LEG-7714.1. Owner: counsel Mira Penn with Trust adjudicator Dev Malik. Dated: 2026-10-20 12:20 UTC. Status: interpretation guidance; no partial-release decision.

The order terms describe one working matched kit for $4,800, with no accepted component prices. Lumen's later allocation may help assess value but does not amend the bargain. DSP-4.0 permits release for separately purchased and priced items, while its treatment of economically divisible bundled kits is undefined. Dev has not issued a decision that $3,000 is undisputed. The buyer disputes a battery as well as the body and describes the working combination as material. A decision relying on retained value would need to explain why the amount left held is adequate and why the buyer's whole-kit allegation does not prevent severability.

Counsel sees a colorable argument that accessories capable of independent resale can have undisputed economic value even in a bundle. Trust sees a counterargument that releasing most of the payable before neutral inventory would undermine the accepted order's integrated promise and leave inadequate refund coverage. Neither view supplies an automatic entitlement to immediate release. Dev owns the policy interpretation under the delegation. If the parties want a voluntary partial compromise instead, both must agree to its consequences and the correct approver must approve it; Lumen's unilateral valuation is not the buyer's consent.

A staff request for review can say which amount and reading are at issue, maintain a safe current hold and name a decision time. It should not represent that the money has been released while waiting for a decision. A provisional customer message should identify an unapproved partial release as a proposal rather than an unconditional entitlement. Operational instructions must preserve the approval requirement. Counsel does not require every pending authority question to prevent any useful communication about logistics, evidence and next updates.

PF-92 is a separate platform-funded acquisition, not an adjudication that either party breached the order. Its conditions and sums cannot be replaced with a smaller seller-funded compromise merely because that looks cheaper. Both owners' consent is to specific terms in E18. Selecting the ordinary process does not force an admission of fraud, and selecting a no-fault acquisition does not require abandoning a request for correction of inaccurate support messaging. The platform should explain these distinctions in plain language without giving either party a false impression that all legal issues have been settled.

Nothing in this note purports to determine statutory rights, a card issuer's future decision or all potential production-loss claims. The internal fourteen-day review procedure can be described accurately without saying it is the exclusive route available in law. Any request for a release of wider claims requires fresh legal review and consent. No such release is authorized here. Privacy limits in E13 remain applicable whether the platform spends its own money or a Trust decision ultimately allocates the loss. Fair process calls for a meaningful factual explanation; it does not require disclosing unrestricted fraud machinery or third-party records.
