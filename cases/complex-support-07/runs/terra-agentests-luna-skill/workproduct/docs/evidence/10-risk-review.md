# E10 — Inconclusive fraud signals and review scope

Record version: RISK-7714.2. Owner: Risk investigator Talia Wynn. Signed: 2026-10-20 10:00 UTC. Status: preliminary triage, no adverse finding.

Risk triage identified two signals requiring context. The buyer's login at claim submission used an address range also observed on another marketplace account with an earlier return claim. Both addresses resolve to a shared production coworking network used by many tenants. The accounts do not share a verified payment instrument or identity record. The signal is therefore insufficient to establish common control, collusion or a fraudulent substitution. The restricted record contains identifiers not needed for a goods dispute, and no permission exists to reveal the other account's name or history to Lumen.

A seller upload was created from an image editor on October 16, after the claim. That file is the lower-resolution packing-table export. Lumen says it cropped a preexisting original for the support form. The original file's recorded creation field is October 13, but metadata can be altered by ordinary copy operations. Image editing alone does not prove fabrication, just as a camera timestamp alone cannot prove uninterrupted packing. The investigator has requested a limited explanation of the export sequence, not unrestricted access to the seller's device or all business photographs.

Risk has not made a finding against either account and has not authorized suspension. No outstanding fraud restriction blocks a platform-funded assistance payment under E15. That absence is a clearance for the specified payment condition, not a certificate that either party is honest or that future evidence cannot change the investigation. The investigator will review the neutral intake photographs and serial comparison on October 21. If a material discrepancy appears, Risk will document the evidence and ask for a response before proposing an account consequence, subject to applicable urgent safety procedures.

The team should explain that conflicting records and their limits are being compared. It can say that no fraud determination has been made. It must not tell the buyer that there was no investigation, or tell the seller that a secret score proved a switch. Useful transparency can identify the kinds of evidence reviewed and the next step without publishing device fingerprints, network correlations, staff thresholds or unrelated user data. Redaction must not be used to hide the existence of a material allegation that a party needs to answer.

Talia's next internal checkpoint is October 21 at 11:30. A short status can be sent today before 16:00, but a final attribution report cannot be promised by then. The risk task remains open if a no-fault commercial resolution is used; any subsequent account enforcement requires its own supported process. Similarly, an inspection route must not become an indefinite payout freeze merely because final fraud attribution takes longer. The goods decision and risk investigation have different questions, evidence thresholds and owners. Reusing a risk flag as an unexplained substitute for adjudication would conflate those questions and deny both parties a useful explanation.
