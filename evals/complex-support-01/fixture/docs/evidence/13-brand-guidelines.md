# E13 — HarborEdge communication standard

Policy version: COM-5.1. Owner: Communications Director. Effective: 2024-01-15. Status: approved customer communication standard, subordinate to truthful account facts and authorized commitments.

Use plain language that helps a customer decide what to do next. A frightening invoice deserves a direct acknowledgment of its size and practical effect. Explain the actual decision early, including whether it has been approved, submitted, posted, or left pending. A sympathetic opening followed by an unexplained maze of departments is not useful ownership. Avoid treating warmth as proof that the financial and operational plan is sound.

When HarborEdge missed a review step, say what failed and what will happen next. Do not hide behind passive abstractions such as a situation having occurred or a system having generated an amount. At the same time, do not invent an admission that the meter was wrong or every notice failed. Accountability can be specific: the exceptional invoice should have been reviewed before release; notice usefulness and traffic cause remain under examination. That distinction is clearer than either blanket blame or blanket exoneration.

The customer may call the event an attack. Staff should acknowledge the concern without adopting an unsupported security conclusion. Explain what the evidence can and cannot show, using everyday language. “A partial sample shows concentrated requests for one file” is more informative than an unexplained label. Avoid naming an attacker, public cloud tenant, or downstream client as responsible without verified evidence and disclosure approval. Do not imply that a routine publishing error makes the customer undeserving of care.

Choose terms with operational meaning. A credit note reduces an invoice. A refund returns money already collected. A collection hold prevents specified collection actions for a stated period. These may need short explanations, but should not be collapsed into “everything is fixed.” Avoid guaranteed dates for bank posting, complete security attribution, or product features outside staff control. A clear commitment to a controlled action with an update time can be strong even while a final outcome remains uncertain.

Sales language should help the customer compare options. Fear of another enormous bill must not become the reason to buy a larger plan that lacks a cap. Correct an earlier broad promise without attacking the colleague who made it or suggesting the customer was foolish to rely on it. A sentence explaining that the upgrade is optional and cannot resolve the existing invoice may be necessary when the quote has already been presented. There is no requirement to include a sales pitch in a crisis response.

Respect the customer's time and authority. Ask only for information needed to resolve a genuine gap, name the staff owner who will obtain internal evidence, and give an update deadline even when a team is waiting. Avoid excessive legal jargon, moral judgments about traffic, threats about publicity, and requests to keep the matter quiet. The standard permits concise replies, detailed technical follow-ups, and different responsible resolutions. It does not require a prescribed apology sentence, maximum cheerfulness, or a claim that the customer will certainly stay.
