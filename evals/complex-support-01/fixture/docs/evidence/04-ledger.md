The remaining open receivable is $103,900. The separate $120 February base subscription was paid February 1 and is not disputed. The billing engine's raw usage calculation is internally reproducible. That fact means the invoice can be explained from recorded counters. It does not prove that the customer understood the exposure, received actionable notice, caused every request, or should bear the commercial outcome. Finance has not certified the event's security classification. The event's transfer number is a billing aggregate; the smaller sampled security dataset must not be presented as a complete request ledger.

An incident-fee cancellation for the full $104,500 requires two linked operations: a $103,900 credit note against the unpaid balance and a $600 refund liability for the collected portion. After the credit note posts, the incident receivable is zero. The refund is then submitted to the original payment method through the processor. The refund liability is not a wallet credit, and no future purchase is required to use it. Staff should not promise the customer's bank will display the return on the submission day.

The processor currently estimates five to ten business days after acceptance for the return to appear. Finance can promise to submit an authorized request by a controlled deadline and provide its reference after acceptance. It cannot guarantee the bank's posting time. A failed processor submission requires a human update and corrected instructions, not an assertion that the money is already back. No chargeback is presently recorded; the team must ask finance to recheck before sending a duplicate cash return if the customer reports one later.
