# E06 — Archived incident settlement desk guide

Policy version: FIN-2.4. Owner: former Billing Operations lead. Approved: 2023-09-12. Effective through: 2024-02-20 for new incident invoices. Status: archived; retained in the support search index for older invoices and audit reconstruction.

The former procedure allowed a support associate to request an incident settlement calculated as a percentage of disputed excess transfer. The associate could propose a review but could not bind finance to a final figure. Its example used a twenty-percent settlement for a small first event, with exceptional reduction to five percent requiring director approval. The examples were negotiation starting points, not an entitlement, debt floor, or mandatory payment schedule. The guide assumed that ordinary metering and notification checks had already occurred.

The desk guide encouraged explaining that increased service capacity has a real operating cost. Its original suggested wording has caused complaints when used before an investigation because it sounds as though technical cost alone decides customer liability. The guide also suggested future service credit for small goodwill cases where the customer intended to remain. It did not authorize converting a settled cash debit into restricted service credit after the customer requested return of funds. Even when this guide applied, an approved financial remedy still needed ledger processing.

An archived appendix describes a manual account pause for hobby projects whose owners explicitly preferred downtime to uncertain usage. That appendix refers to single-site accounts. It does not include agency client continuity requirements, asset-level mitigation, or the later security escalation procedure. Its shorthand “pause after warning” lacks a current consent record and cannot establish that Mara has approved taking six sites down. Staff looking up the old keyword may nevertheless encounter it before the current runbook.

The support index entry has a last-indexed date of February 28, which indicates a search refresh rather than renewed policy approval. The header's effective period remains controlling. A copied paragraph inside a newer ticket retains its original authority and scope; copying does not upgrade a proposal into an executed order. The policy owner asked the search team to add an archive banner, but that change is still pending. Staff should record which version informed a decision rather than infer currency from search position.

For invoices issued by February 20, finance may still consult this guide alongside the applicable signed order. It remains useful evidence of how an earlier associate could have reached a percentage proposal and why the customer may believe that proposal was official. It also explains a real process problem: internal retrieval makes stale material easy to reuse. Recognizing that problem does not excuse repeating an unsupported commitment in the final response.

The current dispute procedure, FIN-3.2, was approved before Aster's invoice date. There is no grandfather clause in this archived guide that preserves a minimum percentage payment for the February 24 invoice. An associate's February 26 suggestion should therefore be explained and corrected through accountable review, without accusing the customer of misunderstanding a perfectly clear message.
