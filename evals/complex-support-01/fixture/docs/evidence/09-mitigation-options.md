# E09 — Site engineering continuity plan

Record version: ENG-482.2. Owner: Site Engineering, Tomas Bell. Prepared: 2024-03-01 11:50 UTC. Status: tested options on a staging copy; no production change executed. Customer authorization scope is recorded in E03.

Option one replaces Linden's large promotional video with a static notice linking to a small accessible text page. The replacement removes the known high-transfer object from the supported deployment and invalidates its ordinary cache key. Engineering can complete it in about thirty minutes after the support owner confirms the deployment identifier. Existing external copies may persist. A versioned or previously cached URL can still be requested until invalidation propagates. This is a meaningful reduction, not a guarantee that spend will immediately become zero.

Mara authorized Leon to replace that asset. Leon has supplied the staging preview and agreed that the message preserves ticket links and accessibility information. Cedar, Beacon, and Tern information pages remain untouched. Engineering should verify the replacement from two regions and record the time. The consent does not authorize removal of the whole festival site, editing downstream client copy beyond the notice, or shutting down the agency account. A failed deployment should roll back only the attempted asset change while the owner receives a status update.

Option two puts the entire agency behind a challenge page and aggressive regional blocking. A staging test reduced automated-looking requests but broke the clinic's text-only appointment instructions for one accessibility tool and prevented a ticket partner's link check. The proposal requires separate approval of the service impact and a review of the affected audiences. It is not the default choice for an account with mixed client obligations. A challenge also cannot be sold as a currency cap; legitimate traffic may continue to consume metered resources.

Option three pauses all deployments and disables public delivery. It would stop most new serving after propagation but take every client page offline, including information Mara explicitly asked to keep available. It may be appropriate only after informed account-owner consent or a documented security emergency. A request to stop payment attempts is not a request to stop serving pages. Billing can suppress incident collection without using a technical outage as leverage.

The current alert system can email a threshold notice and create an internal ticket. It cannot enforce a user-configured dollar ceiling on Agency Basic or Team Plus. Engineering can manually watch account transfer through Monday, but that is a best-effort operational check with an on-call owner, not continuous enforcement or a contractual cap. Any offer to monitor should state the next review time and what action requires consent. A future product roadmap item must not be represented as deployed.

The technically cleanest long-term design would move large festival media to storage with explicit delivery controls and keep lightweight information pages independently available. That migration requires client coordination, export verification, and a separate estimate. It cannot be completed merely by changing the HarborEdge plan. Support should provide this option without forcing an immediate architecture decision during a billing crisis. The present response needs a safe first action and a follow-up owner more than a promise that one purchase fixes every risk.
